Follow one example

A fictional feature request, from a customer idea to a customer update. Pick a step, or open the optional branches.

1. Customers ask

Customers post ideas and vote. Your team moderates. A vote is a signal, not a promise.

Starts in Public OS

Optional branch: Discussion and moderation

Your team merges duplicates and keeps threads on topic.

2. Signals come together

A product manager groups feedback, support tickets and customer notes, then judges what matters. Tools can help organise. A person decides.

Optional link: Public OS → PM OS

PM OS works from your own notes and tickets too. It does not need Public OS.

3. Options weighed

The team compares approaches and records why. Deferring an option is a recorded reason, not a promise.

Inside PM OS

4. Scope agreed

The product lead locks a versioned PRD snapshot: what is in scope, and what is out.

Inside PM OS

Optional branch: Curated roadmap · Public OS

Leaders choose which items customers can see. A roadmap card shows intention, not a launch date.

5. Delivery coordinated

Teams track dependencies and release milestones so people can coordinate. Clarimeld does not build or deploy anything.

Inside PM OS

6. Brief and sources

A writer starts from a brief and pins the sources beside the draft, such as the agreed scope.

Optional link: PM OS → Writer OS

Writer OS projects start from a brief. They do not need a PRD.

7. Draft checked and packed

Advisory flags help editors spot claims outside the sources or scope. The draft can then be packed as an article, notes or an email kit.

Inside Writer OS

Optional branch: Packs without a sign-off

Making a pack does not need a software approval step. Editors still check the claims.

8. Release note published

The release note moves to Public OS. A person reviews it and publishes it. Nothing publishes by itself.

Optional link: Writer OS → Public OS

You can also write a changelog directly in Public OS.

9. You choose who hears

Publishing does not email anyone. Review the eligible recipients, then send.

Optional step in Public OS

Optional branch: Who is eligible

Requesters, followers and eligible recipients are separate groups.

10. Replies come back

Customer replies are logged for the next round of discovery. They do not commit your roadmap.

Optional link: Public OS → PM OS

Optional branch: Not every reply starts new work

A person decides which replies become evidence for a future cycle.

11. Outcomes reviewed

The product team reviews replies and observations against what it expected. It is a review practice, not an automatic report.

Inside PM OS · closes the loop
